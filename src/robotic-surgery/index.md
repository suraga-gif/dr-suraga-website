## How it works

The surgeon sits at a console a few metres from the operating table and looks into a high-definition 3D view of the inside of the body, magnified many times. Hand and foot controls move thin instruments that pass through small cuts. These instruments bend and rotate more freely than a human wrist, and the system filters out the small natural tremor of the hand.

## Possible benefits

For suitable patients, compared with open surgery, robotic surgery can mean:

- Smaller cuts and smaller scars
- Less blood loss
- Less pain after the operation
- A shorter hospital stay and a quicker return to normal activities

Removing the cancer completely always comes first. A robotic operation is chosen only when it allows the same thorough cancer surgery as an open operation.

## Cancers where robotic surgery is often used

- [Rectal and colon cancers](/specialties/colorectal/), where the narrow pelvis makes precision especially valuable
- [Uterine and other gynaecological cancers](/specialties/gynecological-hipec/)
- [Breast cancer](/specialties/breast/), in selected cases, through small cuts hidden in the armpit
- [Oesophagus and stomach cancers](/specialties/upper-gi/), in selected patients
- [Head and neck cancers](/specialties/head-and-neck/), using approaches that keep scars off the neck and face

## Scar-sparing techniques for the head and neck

- **[RIA-MIND](/robotic-surgery/ria-mind/)**: robotic neck dissection through small cuts below the collarbone
- **[PRIA](/robotic-surgery/pria/)**: robotic removal of parotid gland tumours through the same route
