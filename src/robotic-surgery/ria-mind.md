## For patients

### Why the neck lymph nodes are removed

Many cancers of the mouth and throat can spread to the lymph nodes in the neck, sometimes before they can be felt. Removing these nodes is called a **neck dissection**. It treats any spread and tells your team whether more treatment is needed.

### The usual operation, and the difference with RIA-MIND

A conventional neck dissection is done through a long cut across the side of the neck, which leaves a scar that stays visible every day.

With **RIA-MIND**:

- The robotic instruments enter through a few small cuts **below the collarbone**.
- The surgeon works up under the skin of the neck to remove the lymph nodes.
- Clothing covers the small scars, and the neck is left without a visible scar.

### What to expect

- The operation is done under general anaesthesia.
- A small drain is usually placed for a few days.
- Most people return to normal activities sooner than after open surgery, but recovery varies from person to person.

## For referring doctors

### Technique

RIA-MIND is performed with the da Vinci Xi system through ports placed in the infraclavicular region. A working space is created with CO₂ insufflation in the subplatysmal plane, allowing clearance of neck levels I to V from a single access site. Neck dissection can be combined with management of the primary tumour as clinically appropriate.

### Published results

- **Khan et al., 2023:** first description of the technique (*Journal of Minimal Access Surgery*).
- **Ramakrishnan et al., 2026:** retrospective cohort of **62 patients** treated between July 2018 and May 2025 (*Frontiers in Oncology*). Dr. Suraga is a co-author. The study reported:
  - adequate nodal yield
  - no documented complications in 87.1% of patients
  - a local recurrence rate of 8.1%, with no regional (neck) recurrences
  - a structured learning curve on CUSUM analysis

The authors note that longer-term prospective studies are needed.
