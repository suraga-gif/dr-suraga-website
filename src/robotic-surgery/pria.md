## For patients

### What is the parotid gland?

The parotid is the largest salivary gland. It sits in front of and below the ear. A lump here is the most common sign of a parotid tumour.

### The usual operation, and the difference with PRIA

A conventional parotidectomy is done through a cut that runs in front of the ear and down into the neck. **PRIA** uses the same route below the collarbone as [RIA-MIND](/robotic-surgery/ria-mind/):

- Small cuts are made below the collarbone.
- The robotic instruments travel under the skin up to the parotid gland.
- The tumour is removed while the facial nerve is protected.
- Clothing covers the scars, so none are visible on the face or neck.

### What to expect

- The operation is done under general anaesthesia.
- A small drain is usually placed for a short time.
- Your face may feel numb or slightly weak for a while. This is checked at every follow-up visit.

## For referring doctors

### Technique

Four ports are placed in the infraclavicular region and the da Vinci Xi system is docked. A subplatysmal flap is raised to reach the parotid. The facial nerve is identified and preserved, and both superficial and deep lobe tumours can be addressed. A drain is placed at the end of the procedure.

### Referring a patient

Please share the FNAC or biopsy report and the imaging (MRI preferred for deep lobe lesions). See [information for doctors](/for-doctors/).
