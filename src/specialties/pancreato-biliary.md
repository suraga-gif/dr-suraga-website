## Which cancers are included?

- **Pancreatic cancer**
- **Gallbladder cancer**
- **Bile duct cancer** (cholangiocarcinoma)
- **Periampullary cancers**, where the bile duct and pancreas open into the intestine

## Warning signs

- Yellow eyes or skin (jaundice), often with itching
- Dark urine and pale stools
- Pain in the upper abdomen or back
- Losing weight without trying, or loss of appetite
- Diabetes appearing for the first time in later life, along with any of the signs above

## How it is diagnosed

- **Blood tests**, including liver function and the CA 19-9 tumour marker
- **Contrast CT scan** designed to look at the pancreas, and **MRI/MRCP** to look at the bile ducts
- **Endoscopic ultrasound**, which can also take a biopsy
- **ERCP**, an endoscopic procedure to place a stent if jaundice needs to be relieved

## Treatment

When the cancer can be removed, surgery offers the best chance of cure. Common operations are:

- **Whipple operation** for tumours in the head of the pancreas or around the ampulla
- **Distal pancreatectomy** for tumours in the body or tail of the pancreas
- **Radical cholecystectomy** for gallbladder cancer, which removes the gallbladder with a small part of the liver and the nearby lymph nodes

Chemotherapy is often given before or after surgery. Some of these operations can be done with robotic or keyhole techniques in selected patients.

## Recovery

- These are major operations, and the hospital stay is longer than for most cancer surgery.
- Some people need digestive enzyme capsules with meals, and blood sugar checks after pancreatic surgery.
