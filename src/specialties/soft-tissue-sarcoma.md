## What are soft tissue sarcomas?

Sarcomas are uncommon cancers of the body's supporting tissues. They can appear almost anywhere. They are most often found in the thigh, arm, trunk, or deep in the abdomen, in an area called the retroperitoneum.

## Warning signs

- A lump that is getting bigger
- A lump larger than a golf ball (about 5 cm)
- A lump that is deep, under the muscle
- A lump that is painful
- For tumours deep in the abdomen: swelling, a feeling of fullness, or vague discomfort

## Why the first step matters

**A sarcoma should be biopsied and operated on as part of a planned treatment.** If a lump is removed as a simple "lump removal" without the right scans, cancer cells can be left behind. That makes later treatment harder. Scans and the biopsy route should be planned by the surgeon who will do the final operation.

## How it is diagnosed

- **MRI scan** for lumps in the limbs and trunk, and **CT scan** for tumours in the abdomen
- **Core needle biopsy**, planned so the needle track can be removed at surgery
- **CT scan of the chest**, to check the lungs

## Treatment

- **Surgery** removes the tumour with a margin of normal tissue all around it (wide local excision).
- **Radiotherapy**, before or after surgery, lowers the chance of the tumour coming back in the same place.
- **Chemotherapy** is used for some types of sarcoma.

## Recovery and follow-up

- Physiotherapy helps you regain strength and movement.
- Regular scans continue for several years, because sarcomas can come back late.
