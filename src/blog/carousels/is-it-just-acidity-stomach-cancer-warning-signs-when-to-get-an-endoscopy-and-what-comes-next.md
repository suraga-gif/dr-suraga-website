caption: >-
  Most people with stomach cancer don't ignore their symptoms; they misread
  them. Recurring acidity, feeling full after a few bites, a gradual loss of
  appetite: these feel like everyday digestive trouble, so they are treated with
  antacids for months. By the time a proper evaluation happens, the disease is
  often advanced.


  This November, during Stomach Cancer Awareness Month, I've put together a
  short visual guide to help you recognise when ordinary stomach symptoms need a
  closer look. It covers:


  • The warning signs that should never be ignored

  • Who should get an upper GI endoscopy, and when

  • What the next steps are if an endoscopy shows cancer, and why seeing a
  surgical oncologist early matters


  The key message is simple: if stomach symptoms persist beyond 3–4 weeks
  despite medication, get evaluated. An endoscopy is a quick day-care procedure,
  and stomach cancer detected at an early stage can often be treated with
  curative intent.
